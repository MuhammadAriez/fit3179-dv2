How Australian schooling is funded

Australia has three kinds of school. Government schools are run by the states and territories. Catholic and independent schools are non-government: they charge fees, but they also receive public money.

Two levels of government pay for schools: the Australian Government in Canberra, and the eight state and territory governments.

Who pays

In 2023-24, governments spent $91.04 billion on schools. The states and territories paid 68% and the Australian Government paid 32%. But the split depends on which school a child attends.

A decade of change

After inflation, government funding per student rose 25.7% between 2014-15 and 2023-24. For government schools, Australian Government funding per student rose 55.1%, against 22.6% from the states.

Is funding following the students?

Government schools taught 66.8% of students in 2006 and 62.8% in 2025. Independent schools grew from 13.1% to 17.2%.
